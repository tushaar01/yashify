Section G

Porta Hepatis

What is Porta Hepatis?

Definition: Porta hepatis (Latin for "gate of liver") is a transverse fissure on the visceral surface where major vessels, ducts, and nerves enter and exit the liver.

Think of it as: The main gateway or entrance hall of the liver - everything important goes through here!

Location & Appearance

  • Surface: Visceral (inferior) surface of liver
  • Position: Forms the horizontal crossbar of H-shaped fissures
  • Length: About 5 cm long
  • Direction: Transverse (horizontal)
  • Boundaries:
    • Anterior: Quadrate lobe (Segment IVb)
    • Posterior: Caudate lobe (Segment I)
    • Right side: Continues into right lobe
    • Left side: Continues into left lobe
  • Appearance: Deep fissure, reddish due to vascular structures

Structures ENTERING Porta Hepatis

1. Portal Vein

  • Position: Posterior-most structure
  • Division: Divides into right and left branches at porta
  • Function: Brings 70% of liver's blood supply (nutrient-rich, low oxygen)
  • Blood from: Spleen, stomach, intestines, pancreas
  • Size: Largest vessel entering (about 1-1.2 cm diameter)

2. Hepatic Artery Proper

  • Position: Anterior and to the left
  • Division: Divides into right and left hepatic arteries
  • Function: Provides 30% of liver's blood (oxygen-rich)
  • Origin: Common hepatic artery (from celiac trunk)
  • Branches before porta: Gives right gastric and gastroduodenal arteries

3. Nerve Plexus

  • Type: Hepatic nerve plexus (sympathetic and parasympathetic)
  • Sympathetic: From celiac plexus
  • Parasympathetic: From vagus nerve (mainly left vagus)
  • Function: Regulate blood flow and bile secretion

4. Lymphatic Vessels (Afferent)

  • Carry lymph TO hepatic lymph nodes
  • Drain liver tissue
  • Important in metastasis pathways

Structures EXITING Porta Hepatis

1. Right & Left Hepatic Ducts

  • Position: Anterior and to the right
  • Function: Drain bile from right and left lobes
  • Union: Join just outside porta to form common hepatic duct
  • Drainage pattern:
    • Right duct: Drains segments V, VI, VII, VIII
    • Left duct: Drains segments II, III, IV (and I separately)

2. Lymphatic Vessels (Efferent)

  • Drain FROM hepatic lymph nodes
  • Go to celiac nodes
  • Eventually reach cisterna chyli â thoracic duct

Portal Triad - The Three Main Structures

"Portal Triad" refers to three structures that run together:

  1. Portal Vein (Posterior, largest)
  2. Hepatic Artery Proper (Anterior left)
  3. Bile Duct (Anterior right - this forms from hepatic ducts)

These three travel together in hepatoduodenal ligament and branch together throughout liver!

Clinical Importance of Porta Hepatis

1. Surgical Significance

  • Pringle Maneuver: Temporary clamping of hepatoduodenal ligament (portal triad) to control bleeding during liver surgery
  • Hepatectomy: Must carefully dissect and ligate structures at porta when removing liver lobes
  • Liver transplant: Portal structures are anastomosed (connected) here

2. Portal Hypertension

  • Increased pressure in portal vein
  • Causes: Cirrhosis, thrombosis, compression at porta
  • Effects: Varices, splenomegaly, ascites
  • Porta hepatis is key site of obstruction in some cases

3. Cholangiocarcinoma (Hilar Type)

  • Cancer at junction of right and left hepatic ducts (at porta)
  • Also called "Klatskin tumor"
  • Causes obstructive jaundice
  • Difficult to treat due to location

4. Lymph Node Involvement

  • Hepatic lymph nodes located at porta hepatis
  • First site of spread in liver cancer
  • Examined during cancer staging
  • Can be enlarged in various liver diseases

5. Radiological Importance

  • CT/MRI: Portal structures clearly visible
  • Ultrasound: "Mickey Mouse sign" - portal vein (head) with hepatic artery and bile duct (ears)
  • Key landmark in imaging studies
  • Used to identify segments and lobes

6. Bile Duct Obstruction

  • Stones, tumors, or strictures at porta can block bile drainage
  • Causes jaundice (yellowing)
  • May require stenting or surgery

Section H

Blood Supply of Liver

Unique Feature: Dual Blood Supply

The liver is UNIQUE - it receives blood from TWO sources:

Portal Vein (70%)

Nutrient-Rich, Low Oxygen

  • From digestive organs
  • Brings nutrients for processing
  • Deoxygenated blood

Hepatic Artery (30%)

Oxygen-Rich

  • From aorta (via celiac trunk)
  • Provides oxygen for metabolism
  • Oxygenated blood

Why? Liver needs nutrients to process (portal) AND oxygen to work (arterial)!

1. Portal Venous Supply (70% of Blood)

Portal Vein Basics

  • Formation: Behind neck of pancreas, portal vein forms by union of:
    • Superior mesenteric vein (SMV)
    • Splenic vein
    • At level of L2 vertebra
  • Length: About 8 cm long
  • Position: Ascends behind first part of duodenum
  • Course: Travels in free edge of lesser omentum (hepatoduodenal ligament)
  • Termination: Divides at porta hepatis into right and left branches

Branching Pattern

Portal Vein (main trunk)

â Divides at Porta Hepatis â
Right Portal Vein
  • Shorter, wider
  • Supplies right lobe (Segments V, VI, VII, VIII)
  • Divides into:
    • Anterior branch: Segments V, VIII
    • Posterior branch: Segments VI, VII
Left Portal Vein
  • Longer, narrower
  • Has horizontal and vertical parts (L-shaped)
  • Supplies left lobe + quadrate + caudate
  • Divides into:
    • Lateral branch: Segments II, III
    • Medial branch: Segment IV (a and b)
    • Caudate branches: Segment I
  • Ligamentum teres connects to left portal vein

What Blood Contains

  • Nutrients: Absorbed from intestines (glucose, amino acids, fats)
  • Toxins: Absorbed from gut (need detoxification)
  • Hormones: Pancreatic hormones (insulin, glucagon)
  • Red blood cells: Old RBCs from spleen for breakdown
  • Oxygen: Low (about 40% saturated) - already used by intestines

Tributaries of Portal Vein

Portal vein collects blood from:

  • Superior mesenteric vein: Small intestine, cecum, ascending and transverse colon
  • Splenic vein: Spleen, pancreas, stomach (via left gastric vein)
  • Left gastric vein: Stomach (lesser curvature, esophagus)
  • Right gastric vein: Stomach (lesser curvature)
  • Cystic vein: Gallbladder (joins right portal branch)
  • Para-umbilical veins: Around umbilicus (in ligamentum teres)

2. Hepatic Arterial Supply (30% of Blood)

Arterial Supply Basics

  • Source: Aorta â Celiac trunk â Common hepatic artery â Hepatic artery proper
  • Function: Provides oxygen-rich blood for liver metabolism
  • Percentage: 30% of total blood but 50% of liver's oxygen supply

Arterial Pathway

1. Celiac Trunk
  • First branch of abdominal aorta
  • At level of T12 vertebra
  • Divides into 3 branches:
    • Left gastric artery
    • Splenic artery
    • Common hepatic artery â This goes to liver
2. Common Hepatic Artery
  • Runs along superior border of pancreas
  • Goes towards liver
  • Branches:
    • Gastroduodenal artery: To stomach and duodenum
    • Right gastric artery: To stomach
    • After giving these â becomes hepatic artery proper
3. Hepatic Artery Proper
  • Ascends in hepatoduodenal ligament
  • Reaches porta hepatis
  • Divides into right and left hepatic arteries
  • Position in portal triad: Anterior and to the left
4. Right Hepatic Artery
  • Supplies: Right lobe (Segments V, VI, VII, VIII)
  • Branch: Cystic artery to gallbladder (very important!)
  • Course: Passes posterior to common hepatic duct (usually)
  • Variation: May arise from superior mesenteric artery (in 10-15% people)
5. Left Hepatic Artery
  • Supplies: Left lobe, quadrate, caudate lobes (Segments I, II, III, IV)
  • Course: Runs in fissure for ligamentum venosum
  • Smaller than right hepatic artery

Anatomical Variations (Important for Surgery!)

Normal pattern in only 60-70% of people! Common variations:

  • Replaced right hepatic artery: Arises from superior mesenteric artery (10-15%)
  • Replaced left hepatic artery: Arises from left gastric artery (10-15%)
  • Accessory hepatic arteries: Extra arteries alongside normal ones (10%)
  • Complete replacement: Both right and left arise from different sources (rare)

Surgical Importance: Must identify variations before surgery to avoid accidentally cutting blood supply!

3. Venous Drainage

Hepatic Veins

Blood exits liver through hepatic veins (NOT through portal vein!)

The Three Main Hepatic Veins

Right Hepatic Vein

  • Drains: Right posterior and right anterior sectors
    • Segments VI, VII (posterior)
    • Segments V, VIII (anterior)
  • Course: Runs between anterior and posterior sectors
  • Entry to IVC: Most posterior
  • Size: Largest hepatic vein

Middle Hepatic Vein

  • Drains: Right anterior sector + Left medial sector
    • Segments V, VIII (right side)
    • Segment IV a, b (left side)
  • Course: Runs in Cantlie's line (midline of liver, right-left division)
  • Entry to IVC: Middle position
  • Special: Often joins with left hepatic vein before IVC

Left Hepatic Vein

  • Drains: Left lateral sector
    • Segments II, III
  • Course: Runs between left medial and lateral sectors
  • Entry to IVC: Most anterior
  • Junction: Often joins middle hepatic vein
Additional Small Hepatic Veins
  • Inferior right hepatic veins: Small veins from right lobe directly to IVC
  • Caudate lobe veins: Segment I drains DIRECTLY into IVC (multiple small veins)
  • Number: Variable, usually 5-20 small veins in addition to the 3 main ones

Clinical Significance of Blood Supply

1. Hepatic Artery Occlusion

  • Cause: Thrombosis, surgical accident, trauma
  • Result: Liver infarction (tissue death) - serious!
  • Why serious: Liver depends on arterial oxygen (portal vein has low oxygen)
  • Segments affected: Depends on which branch is occluded

2. Portal Vein Thrombosis

  • Cause: Cirrhosis, cancer, clotting disorders
  • Result: Portal hypertension, varices
  • Less severe than arterial occlusion (collaterals can form)
  • Treatment: Anticoagulation, shunt surgery

3. Budd-Chiari Syndrome

  • Definition: Hepatic vein thrombosis (blocked drainage)
  • Result: Liver congestion, pain, hepatomegaly
  • Unique: Caudate lobe SPARED (drains directly to IVC)
  • Imaging: Caudate lobe enlargement characteristic

4. Cystic Artery in Surgery

  • Origin: Usually from right hepatic artery
  • Importance: Must be ligated during cholecystectomy (gallbladder removal)
  • Variation: May have aberrant origin
  • Caution: Calot's triangle dissection to identify safely

5. Pringle Maneuver

  • Technique: Clamp hepatoduodenal ligament
  • Effect: Stops portal vein + hepatic artery blood flow
  • Purpose: Control bleeding during liver surgery
  • Time limit: Maximum 20-30 minutes safely

6. Segmental Resection

  • Surgeons can remove individual segments
  • Each segment has own arterial and portal supply
  • Ligate vessels to that segment only
  • Other segments continue functioning normally
